# Brushless FPV quads (Betaflight/INAV) with 3D-printed frames: printable frames, phone control, phone video, phone configuration, 2026 costs

Research date: 2026-10-05. Popularity numbers for Printables/MakerWorld models were pulled from the Printables GraphQL API and the MakerWorld design API on 2026-10-05 and are cited to each model page. Store prices are from the stores' own product/search endpoints on 2026-10-05 (RaceDayQuads = RDQ, betafpv.com, speedybee.com, radiomasterrc.com). "BNF" = bind-and-fly ready-made quad. Note: the session's web-search budget ran out near the end, so a few items are listed under Gaps instead of being verified.

## 1. Which well-regarded printable brushless frames exist (TPU/PETG whoops, printed 2"–3" cinewhoops, toothpicks, fully printed 3"–5", hybrids)?

### Takeaway
There are many printable frames, but most have few documented "makes". The most validated are small (2"–3.5") frames and 4" unibody designs. For 3" and up, designers mostly recommend stiff engineering filaments (PC, PC-CF, PETG-CF, PAHT-CF). Whoop frames can be printed, but molded whoop frames cost only $1–5 and are often lighter and stronger, so printing a whoop frame is about customisation, not saving money. The PickleWhoop, a frequently cited "printable" cinewhoop, is actually a carbon frame. Only its TPU accessories are printed.

### Inferences
- The user's filaments map onto the designs like this:
  - **TPU 65D** is close to the "TPU for AMS 68D" that the 2" TPU frame specifies. It should suit TPU-specific micro frames, canopies and camera mounts.
  - **TPU 85A** is much softer than the 95A used for canopies and camera mounts in MFR and Quentin, and softer than the 64D–75D used for the TPU whoop frame (which already "flexed a bit much"). It is best kept to bumpers, battery straps and antenna mounts.
  - **PLA/PETG** are fine for whoops (PEP85), 2.5"–3" cinewhoops (Quentin, ÄLVA) and prototypes.
  - **4"–5" frames** are better in CF-filled nylon or PC, given the Crab and G4ZO test notes.
- For a beginner the best-supported printable options are:
  - a PEP85-style 85 mm frame that reuses CetusX-pattern parts;
  - Whifflepick, MFR or kaschberle 2" for small 1S/2S builds;
  - ÄLVA or Quentin for printed cinewhoops;
  - Crab or BM Aether 4 for 4"–5".
  The latter two still need CF-filled filament to survive crashes.
- "Makes" counts on Printables are low (mostly 0–11), so community flight validation is thin. MakerWorld "prints" counts show printing, not necessarily flying.

## 2. What phone-control methods exist for Betaflight/INAV quads (ESP32 bridges, ELRS options, MSP over BLE, gamepads, off-the-shelf), and how mature are they?

### Takeaway
Every phone-to-flight-controller path found is a hobby or experimental project. There is no maintained, mainstream app that flies a Betaflight or INAV quad from a phone. The documented routes are:
- ESP32 Wi-Fi/ESP-NOW bridges that output iBUS/CRSF, or MSP RC;
- ESP-FC, an ESP32 flight controller with a built-in ESP-NOW receiver;
- MAVLink serial RX. INAV 8+ listens for RC_CHANNELS_OVERRIDE. The ExpressLRS docs list a MAVLink serial-RX provider for Betaflight 2025.12+ too, but which messages it accepts is not verified.

ELRS's phone-facing features (Wi-Fi web UI, Backpack, Wi-Fi/BLE sim joystick, MAVLink forwarding) are for configuration, telemetry or simulators, not for flying from a phone. SpeedyBee's built-in Bluetooth is documented as configuration-only.

### Cited Findings
**(a) ESP32/ESP8266 bridges: phone → Wi-Fi/ESP-NOW → FC**
- Michal Schwarz (2019): an ESP32 hosts a web page with an on-screen joystick and sends "Serial-based reciever + IBUS" to an Asgard32 F7 running Betaflight on an F450 frame. "Maximum range is about 50 meters without external antenna", and the author notes "WiFi was not designed for real time robust control applications". — [michalschwarz.github.io](https://michalschwarz.github.io/rc-controller/esp32/quadcopter/f450/asgard32/schema/2019/05/07/esp32-drone-v1.0.0.html)
- Ishu1519 "ESP-FC-ESP-NOW-Web-RC":
  - Chain: phone browser touch UI (WebSocket) → ESP32 transmitter → ESP-NOW → ESP32 running ESP-FC v0.2.1.
  - Rates: 8 channels at 50 Hz over ESP-NOW; phone to transmitter at 40 Hz.
  - Failsafe triggers when packets stop for more than 400 ms (a "500 ms Fail-Safe Watchdog").
  - Range: "400+ m observed line-of-sight".
  - The README says touchscreen control is "strictly intended for ground testing, robotics, rovers, and controlled bench demonstration" and "PROPELLERS MUST BE REMOVED during … wireless link debugging".
  - It has 9 commits.
  - Source: [GitHub](https://github.com/Ishu1519/ESP-FC-ESP-NOW-Web-RC)
- Boyyt357 "ESP32-ESPNOW-RC-Transmitter-Receiver": an ESP-NOW receiver outputs iBUS on one pin into a Betaflight/INAV UART. Phone control needs a separate "ESP32 WiFi Joystick to iBUS Translator". Range is "200−400m" ("Up to 1 km" with an external antenna), timing is set by `delay(20)`, and no failsafe is documented. It has 11 stars and 9 commits. — [GitHub](https://github.com/Boyyt357/ESP32-ESPNOW-RC-Transmitter-Receiver)
- MustafaQassmieh01 "ESP32-Communication-to-crsf" (bachelor-thesis code): mobile app → ESP32 → ESP-NOW → ESP32 → CRSF frames into Betaflight. It was tested on an "OmnibusF4SD … with Betaflight build 2026.6.1" on a 7" platform. The receiver applies "HOVER failsafe threshold: 7 s", "LAND failsafe threshold: 30 s" and a latched KILL, with about 50 Hz output. — [GitHub](https://github.com/MustafaQassmieh01/ESP32-Communication-to-crsf)
- ESP-FC (rtlopez):
  - It is an ESP32/ESP32-S3 flight-controller firmware with a "Builtin ESP-NOW receiver and WiFi configuration". It is compatible with Betaflight Configurator v10.10 and has ACRO/ANGLE/AIRMODE.
  - It warns "this software is not the same as Betaflight".
  - ESP8266 support is "obsolete, no longer developed".
  - Source: [GitHub rtlopez/esp-fc](https://github.com/rtlopez/esp-fc)
- ESP-FC wireless limits:
  - "WiFi function can only be used to configure device. Whilst WiFi is active, it is not possible to ARM controller."
  - The ESP-NOW receiver needs a self-built transmitter ("there are no real transmitters using this protocol on the market"), e.g. espnow-rclink-tx in a radio's JR bay.
  - Wi-Fi is an open AP "ESP-FC" at tcp://192.168.4.1:1111, which switches on after 30 s without a receiver signal.
  - Source: [esp-fc docs/wireless.md](https://github.com/rtlopez/esp-fc/blob/master/docs/wireless.md)
- An Elektor guide (2025-11-30) runs ESP-FC with a XIAO ESP32S3 running espnow-rclink-tx in a radio's JR bay (PPM). It reports "roughly 200 meters … via ESP-NOW" against "several kilometers" with an ELRS receiver (Happymodel EP1, CRSF). — [Elektor Labs](https://www.elektormagazine.com/labs/fly-your-esp32-drone-on-betaflight-with-radio-controller-esp-fc-guide)
- Betaflight 2026.6:
  - "Betaflight now runs on ESP32 microcontrollers" (ESP32-S3 primary, ESP32-WROOM too), marked experimental, with CRSF/S.BUS/GHST/IBUS/FPORT receivers.
  - "Betaflight Bridge" turns an ESP32-S3 into a "USB-host-to-Wi-Fi bridge". It listens on TCP 5761, serves one app client at a time, and is aimed at iOS ("iPhones have no USB serial"). It is a configuration link, not an RC link.
  - Source: [Betaflight 2026.6 release notes](https://betaflight.com/docs/wiki/release/Betaflight-2026-6-Release-Notes)
  - Releases: firmware 2026.6.1 on 2026-08-07 and 2026.6.2 on 2026-09-16. — [betaflight releases feed](https://github.com/betaflight/betaflight/releases.atom)
- Betaflight MSP RC (official guide):
  - `MSP_SET_RAW_RC` (code 200), 1000–2000 µs values, sent at "25 to 50 Hz".
  - Using MSP as the sole receiver (`feature RX_MSP`) is labelled bench-only: "If the companion process stops sending … the flight controller drops into failsafe."
  - The flight-safe pattern is MSP OVERRIDE, where a real receiver keeps ARM and the switch, and "ARM must not sit on an MSP-driven channel".
  - In 4.5.x and 2025.12.x, override values "are held indefinitely" if frames stop. From 2026.6 they fall back to the receiver after 300 ms.
  - Source: [Betaflight MSP Companion Computer Control](https://betaflight.com/docs/wiki/guides/current/MSP-Companion-Computer-Control)
- MAVLink as receiver: the ExpressLRS docs say to set Receiver type Serial and provider MAVLINK on Betaflight (which needs "Betaflight 2025.12.0-beta or later") and on INAV (8+). For INAV, "full functionality and configuration via MAVLink should not be expected". — [ExpressLRS MAVLink](https://www.expresslrs.org/software/mavlink/)
- INAV's "RC over MAVLink" "only listens for `RC_CHANNELS_OVERRIDE` messages" (INAV 8.1 discussion, answered 2026-06-02). — [iNavFlight/inav discussion #11052](https://github.com/iNavFlight/inav/discussions/11052)
- INAV 10.0.0-RC1 (2026-09-24) lists "Improved MAVLink implementation — RC override over MAVLink serial RX, MSP-over-MAVLink tunnel, mission upload/download". RC2 followed on 2026-09-29, and INAV 9.1.0 on 2026-07-11. — [INAV releases feed](https://github.com/iNavFlight/inav/releases.atom)
- DroneBridge for ESP32: "A secure & transparent telemetry link with support for WiFi and ESP-NOW. Supporting MAVLink, MSP, LTM". It offers serial-to-Wi-Fi, Wi-Fi LR, ESP-NOW and BLE, with "Up to 150m range using standard WiFi" and "Up to 1km … using ESP-NOW or Wi-Fi LR". It is supported by QGroundControl, Mission Planner and mwptools. The README does not mention RC control. — [GitHub DroneBridge/ESP32](https://github.com/DroneBridge/ESP32)

**(b) ExpressLRS-related options**
- The ELRS feature list includes "Up to 1000 Hz Packet Rate", "Wifi Updates", "Bluetooth or WiFi Sim Joystick" and a MAVLink receiver protocol. — [GitHub ExpressLRS](https://github.com/ExpressLRS/ExpressLRS). ### Inferences
- The most practical "phone flies the quad" builds today:
  1. **ESP-FC on an ESP32 micro** with a phone-browser ESP-NOW transmitter (Ishu1519 pattern). ESP-FC supports DShot, but the documented builds are small micros (e.g. Elektor's 50 mm quad), and it is not Betaflight proper.
  2. **A normal Betaflight or INAV FC** with an ESP32 receiver that turns phone joystick packets into iBUS or CRSF (Schwarz, Boyyt357, Mustafa).
  3. **INAV 8+/10 or Betaflight 2025.12+ with MAVLink serial RX**, fed RC_CHANNELS_OVERRIDE from a phone app through a Wi-Fi-to-serial bridge such as DroneBridge.

  None is turnkey, and options 1 and 2 put the flight-critical link on 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi or ESP-NOW with home-made failsafe logic.
- The safest phone-assisted pattern Betaflight documents is MSP OVERRIDE: a real ELRS receiver keeps ARM and the mode switch, and the phone or ESP32 only overrides sticks while a switch is engaged. On 2026.6+ it falls back to the receiver after 300 ms.
- SpeedyBee BLE (and Betaflight BLE on iOS) should be treated as configuration-only. Betaflight could in principle accept MSP RC over that UART, but no app was found that does it, and Betaflight labels MSP-as-sole-receiver bench-only.

### Gaps
- No source confirmed whether QGroundControl's Android on-screen joystick sends RC_CHANNELS_OVERRIDE (which INAV needs) or MANUAL_CONTROL. A "QGC on phone flies INAV" setup is therefore unverified.
- No maintained Android/iOS app was found that drives an ELRS TX module over USB-OTG or BLE. kaack's tool is desktop/RPi only.
- Built-in BLE behaviour while armed on SpeedyBee FCs (as opposed to the adapter) was not confirmed.
- Betaflight Bridge documentation beyond the release notes was not found.

## 3. Is phone-only control safe/viable for brushless quads, and what hybrid setups are recommended?

### Takeaway
Phone-only control of a brushless FPV quad is technically possible but not recommended. Wi-Fi links reach roughly 50–150 m and are "not designed for real time robust control". Touchscreens give no tactile stick feel or self-centring and don't build transferable skill. Every phone-control project found either limits itself to bench use or adds its own failsafe. Betaflight's default failsafe ends in a motor-cut drop. The broadly supported approach is a hybrid: a cheap ELRS radio ($33–72) does the flying, and the phone is used for video, spectating, configuration and simulators.

### Cited Findings
- Betaflight failsafe:
  - Signal loss is detected as "no incoming data packets".
  - Stage 1 (guard) is "1.5 seconds in 4.5 (1.0s in 4.4)".
  - The default Stage 2 is DROP: "Just kill the motors and disarm (crash the craft)".
  - "Always ensure that the receiver is configured to 'send no data' on signal loss!"
  - Source: [Betaflight Failsafe](https://betaflight.com/docs/wiki/guides/current/Failsafe)
- Betaflight treats MSP as the sole receiver as bench-only. A stalled companion "drops into failsafe", and ARM "must not sit on an MSP-driven channel". — [Betaflight MSP guide](https://betaflight.com/docs/wiki/guides/current/MSP-Companion-Computer-Control)
- Wi-Fi range and fitness: an ESP32 web joystick gave a "Maximum range … about 50 meters", and "WiFi was not designed for real time robust control applications" ([Schwarz](https://michalschwarz.github.io/rc-controller/esp32/quadcopter/f450/asgard32/schema/2019/05/07/esp32-drone-v1.0.0.html)). DroneBridge gives "Up to 150m … standard WiFi", up to 1 km with ESP-NOW or Wi-Fi LR ([DroneBridge](https://github.com/DroneBridge/ESP32)). ESP-NOW gave about 200 m against "several kilometers" with ELRS ([Elektor](https://www.elektormagazine.com/labs/fly-your-esp32-drone-on-betaflight-with-radio-controller-esp-fc-guide)).
- The phone-browser ESP-NOW project restricts its touchscreen control to ground testing and requires props off while debugging the link. — [Ishu1519](https://github.com/Ishu1519/ESP-FC-ESP-NOW-Web-RC)
- ESP-FC will not arm while its Wi-Fi is active. — [esp-fc wireless.md](https://github.com/rtlopez/esp-fc/blob/master/docs/wireless.md)
- ELRS runs up to 1000 Hz ([ExpressLRS](https://github.com/ExpressLRS/ExpressLRS)). A tutorial cites ELRS command latency "as little as 5ms" and range of "several kilometres" ([CircuitDigest](https://circuitdigest.com/tutorial/how-to-connect-the-litewing-esp32-drone-betaflight)).
- On touch and gamepad control, Oscar Liang writes: "While you can control the drone using the touchscreen, this method doesn't effectively translate to real-world flying skills." He recommends a real radio "to build muscle memory". — [Oscar Liang](https://oscarliang.com/fpv-simulator/)
- Android-based radios run into OS issues ("Long boot times", "Higher battery consumption", "Greater risk of crashes or lag"). — [Oscar Liang AX12](https://oscarliang.com/radiomaster-ax12/)
- Phone video links are also limited. APFPV is "Not suitable for racing or fast aerobatics" ([OpenIPC APFPV](https://docs.openipc.org/use-cases/fpv/apfpv/apfpv/)). Analog OTG receivers at about 100 ms are best reserved "for slow ground models, spectators and bench tests" ([Unmanned Tech](https://www.unmannedtechshop.co.uk/blogs/knowledge-base/eachine-rotg02-android-fpv-receiver-setup-review)).
- Cheap ELRS radios for the hybrid approach:
  - BetaFPV LiteRadio 3 ELRS: $32.99 ([BetaFPV](https://betafpv.com/products/literadio-3-radio-transmitter))
  - BetaFPV LiteRadio 4 ELRS: $59.99 ([RDQ](https://www.racedayquads.com/products/betafpv-literadio-4-radio-transmitter-elrs-2-4ghz))
  - RadioMaster Pocket (M2): $59.99–$71.50, EdgeTX, ELRS 2.4 GHz, 2×18650. The EU LBT variant is limited to 100 mW. ([RadioMaster](https://radiomasterrc.com/products/pocket-radio-controller-m2); [RDQ $71.49](https://www.racedayquads.com/products/radiomaster-pocket-edgetx-rc-transmitter-choose-version))
- A radio plugs into Android over USB-OTG for simulators. On iPhone, "you can connect a radio … wirelessly via WiFi, provided your radio has an ExpressLRS transmitter module". — [Oscar Liang](https://oscarliang.com/fpv-simulator/)

### Inferences
- Recommended hybrid for this user:
  - ELRS radio (LiteRadio 3 ELRS or RadioMaster Pocket) plus an ELRS receiver or AIO on the quad, for control and failsafe.
  - Phone for video: Android analog OTG receiver or OpenIPC/PixelPilot, ideally for a spectator or line-of-sight practice, with goggles for real FPV.
  - Phone for configuration: SpeedyBee app, Betaflight App, ELRS web UI.
  - Phone plus the same radio for simulator practice.
- If the user still wants a phone-flown experiment, keep it to a tiny whoop or an ESP-FC micro indoors or at short range. Use MSP OVERRIDE or a separate real-receiver ARM switch where possible, and test failsafe props-off first.
- Radio-law constraints (5.8 GHz power, EU 100 mW LBT) affect range choices and are covered by another researcher.

### Gaps
- No quantitative measurements were found of end-to-end phone touchscreen → Wi-Fi → FC control latency or jitter.
- The planned Reddit, IntoFPV and Joshua Bardwell quotes on "phone as transmitter" could not be gathered before the search budget ran out.

## 4. What phone video options exist (analog OTG, toy Wi-Fi cams, OpenIPC/PixelPilot, WFB-ng/FPVue, DJI/Walksnail/HDZero), with latency, cost and limitations?

### Takeaway
**Android** is the phone platform for FPV video:
- **Analog 5.8 GHz USB-OTG receivers** (ROTG01/02, Skydroid) are the cheapest ($13–45). They give 640×480 at about 100 ms, which is fine for spectators and bench work but poor for flying.
- **OpenIPC with PixelPilot** gives HD at about 35–70 ms, with an RTL8812AU/EU adapter or, in APFPV mode, the phone's own Wi-Fi at short range.
- **DJI O3/O4** reaches a phone only through DJI Goggles 3/N3 and the DJI Fly app.
- **Walksnail and HDZero** have no phone path except their goggles' or VRX's HDMI output into a capture device or HDMI-in tablet, which adds delay.

**iPhones cannot use UVC OTG receivers.**

### Cited Findings
**Analog USB-OTG receivers (Android)**
- The Eachine ROTG02 scans 150 channels (5645–5945 MHz) with true diversity. "Eachine quotes latency around 100ms", and the reviewer would "reserve it for slow ground models, spectators and bench tests".
  - It needs a phone with USB OTG and UVC: "A USB-C socket alone proves neither."
  - iPhone: "No … It is designed for Android devices that support USB OTG and UVC."
  - Source: [Unmanned Tech review](https://www.unmannedtechshop.co.uk/blogs/knowledge-base/eachine-rotg02-android-fpv-receiver-setup-review)
- 2020 prices: ROTG01 about $13 and ROTG02 about $21. All share 640×480 or 352×288 resolution and draw 200 mA (single) or 300 mA (dual). The author says "I would not recommend to fly via this feed". — [brushlesswhoop.com (2020-02-23)](https://brushlesswhoop.com/usb-otg-fpv-receivers/)
- A ROTG02 review gives "About 100 ms latency", says "iOS based devices are not supported", and notes it records DVR at 640×480 (six minutes ≈ 280 MB). Score 3.6/5, recommended as "a second FPV display". — [FirstQuadcopter](https://www.firstquadcopter.com/reviews/eachine-rotg02-review/)
- The Skydroid 150CH true-diversity UVC receiver is $44.49 and sold out at RDQ. Specs are 640×480 at 30 fps and 190 mA.
  - Reviewers say the Skydroid app is unreliable ("Use the app OTGView instead") and estimate latency as "probably under 100ms".

  - Source: [RDQ](https://www.racedayquads.com/products/skydroid-150ch-5-8ghz-true-diversity-fpv-receiver-module-for-smartphone-usb-otg)

**Digital: OpenIPC + PixelPilot (Android)**
- PixelPilot is "An Android app packaging multiple pieces together to decode an H264/H265 video feed broadcast by wfb-ng". It builds on FPVue_android by Gee He, devourer and wfb-ng.
  - Adapter filter: RTL8812AU and RTL8812EU.
  - Runs on arm64/armv7 Android, including Meta Quest.
  - Features DVR, adaptive link and MediaPipe object detection. Known issue: "Audio stream is not working".
  - Source: [GitHub OpenIPC/PixelPilot](https://github.com/OpenIPC/PixelPilot)
- Latest releases: v0.25.0 and v0.24.0 on 2026-09-02, v0.23.1 on 2025-08-08. — [PixelPilot releases feed](https://github.com/OpenIPC/PixelPilot/releases.atom)
- Requirements: "USB OTG support; the faster the CPU, the lower the latency". RTL8812AU is recommended; RTL8812EU2 may need a powered USB hub. OSD shows MSP telemetry from the FC, and DVR saves MP4 to the phone. — [openfpv.com.ua PixelPilot](https://openfpv.com.ua/en/software/pixelpilot)
- Quick start: "RunCam WiFiLink v1/v2" (IMX415/SSC338Q; v2 has a built-in EU2) plus "PixelPilot on Android — cheapest and fastest way to get flying". PixelPilot latency is about 50–70 ms. — [openfpv.com.ua quick start](https://openfpv.com.ua/en/quick-start/)
- APFPV mode turns the VTX into a Wi-Fi access point ("your VTX simply acts like a WiFi router"), so the phone needs no monitor-mode adapter. It is viewed in PixelPilot or a browser at 192.168.0.1.
  - Latency: "35–70ms".
  - Range: basic smartphone 50–200 m; good adapter 200–500 m.
  - "Only one WiFi device as a receiver is possible" and it is "Not suitable for racing or fast aerobatics".
  - Source: [OpenIPC APFPV guide](https://docs.openipc.org/use-cases/fpv/apfpv/apfpv/)

  - VTX, 200 mW, $89.99: IMX415, 1080p90/720p120, 2–6S, 25.5×25.5 mm, 13.76 g. ([RDQ](https://www.racedayquads.com/products/emax-wyvern-link-openipc-200mw-vtx))
  - Alpha VRX: $51.99. ([RDQ](https://www.racedayquads.com/products/emax-wyvern-link-alpha-openipc-100mw-vrx))
  - Goggle VRX with HDMI out and DVR (Alpha): $129.49. ([RDQ](https://www.racedayquads.com/products/emax-wyvern-link-openipc-goggle-vrx))
- WFB-ng/FPVue: PixelPilot credits FPVue_android (Gee He) as the basis it integrates. — [PixelPilot README](https://github.com/OpenIPC/PixelPilot)

**Other DIY Wi-Fi video**
- "HX-ESP32-CAM-FPV" (RomanLut) is an open-source digital FPV system on ESP32-CAM.
  - "latency 90-110ms", up to 1 km at 24 Mbps.

### Inferences
- On a budget with an Android phone, a 5.8 GHz OTG receiver ($13–45) lets a friend watch, or lets the pilot check video on the bench. For real FPV flying, analog goggles ($75–160) or a digital system are better.

- Toy-style Wi-Fi cameras would have latency at least as high as these Wi-Fi systems and are not recommended (inference only; see Gaps).

Use it for firmware updates and to join home Wi-Fi. — [ELRS Wi-Fi updating](https://www.expresslrs.org/software/updating/wifi-updating/)
- ESP-FC configuration: Betaflight Configurator over TCP to tcp://192.168.4.1:1111 (AP "ESP-FC"). — [esp-fc wireless.md](https://github.com/rtlopez/esp-fc/blob/master/docs/wireless.md)
- Telemetry or ground station on a phone: DroneBridge ESP32 bridges MAVLink, MSP and LTM to QGroundControl, Mission Planner and mwptools ([DroneBridge](https://github.com/DroneBridge/ESP32)). The ELRS TX Backpack forwards MAVLink to a ground station on UDP 14550 ([ELRS MAVLink](https://www.expresslrs.org/software/mavlink/)).

### Inferences
- For a beginner the simplest phone-configurable setup is:
  - a SpeedyBee FC with BLE, configured from the SpeedyBee app; or
  - any FC with an Android phone running the Betaflight App over USB-OTG;
  - plus the ELRS web UI for the receiver.
- iPhone users should expect to rely on the SpeedyBee app (BLE). The Betaflight App on iOS is TestFlight-only and needs a Betaflight Bridge, or BLE from 2026.6.1.

### Gaps
- Not verified: whether the Betaflight PWA's Web Serial works in Chrome for Android.
- INAV has no first-party mobile configurator documented. Only the SpeedyBee app's INAV support was confirmed.
- Not checked: Android SpeedyBee app version and rating.

## 6. What does a typical 2026 bill of materials cost for a printed 1S/2S whoop, a printed 3" and a printed 5"?

### Takeaway
At October 2026 US retail, with filament cost negligible:
- **Printed 1S whoop:** about $145 in parts, plus radio and video. That is more than a ready-made Air65 II or Meteor65 Pro II BNF at $99.99.
- **Printed 3" analog:** about $200 in electronics, or about $315 with four batteries and a charger.
- **Printed 5" analog:** about $275 in electronics, or about $500 with four 6S packs and a charger.

Add an ELRS radio ($33–72) and a video display: phone OTG receiver $13–45, analog goggles $75–160, OpenIPC VTX $90 plus adapter, or DJI O4 + Goggles N3 at about $389.
