# LiPo battery shipping to Australia (Sydney): buyer reports, store policies and the transport rules (state as of 9 Oct 2026)

Scope note: covers loose ("stand-alone") small LiPo packs, such as 1S whoop or toy packs, ordered from overseas stores and delivered to Sydney. Live shipping-rate tests were run on 2026-10-09 by me, against each Shopify store's public `/cart/shipping_rates.json` endpoint, with a battery-only cart and the address set to Sydney, New South Wales 2000. First-hand reports are labelled **[1st-hand]**. Claims repeated from others or not checked are labelled **[2nd-hand]**. Statements by a seller or authority are labelled **[official]**.

## Q1. Buyer reports: do loose LiPos ordered from AliExpress / Banggood / BetaFPV etc. reach Australia, how long do they take, which carrier is used, and do sellers cancel?

### Takeaway
I found no first-hand report from 2023–2026 of a 1S or other small drone LiPo ordered from AliExpress, Banggood or BetaFPV to Sydney. Reddit could not be reached (see Gaps), and that is where such reports usually are. The recent first-hand evidence I could reach covers lithium battery packs and cells in general (e-bike packs, 18650s) bought on AliExpress in 2023–2025. Those orders **arrived**, mostly after **about 1–2 months**. The seller said the pack would go **by sea**. One 2023 batch arrived in 11 days. Older reports (2017–2021) describe Banggood and Gearbest refusing to ship spare LiPos to Australia at checkout, and Australian buyers struggling to find small 1S packs.

### Inferences
- The recent Australian evidence I could reach points to "it arrives, but slowly" for AliExpress lithium battery orders. Typical times were about 4–8 weeks, consistent with sea freight or slow battery lines, with occasional fast arrivals (11 days in Sep 2023). None of the 2023–2025 posts I read reported a seizure or a seller cancellation. These posts are about larger packs and 18650 cells, not 1S drone packs. They do show that AliExpress sellers route batteries to Australia by non-postal (sea) lines.
- Historically (2017), the failure mode was **refusal at checkout** ("unable to ship to Australia"), not seizure in Australia. Commenters then and in 2025 agreed the problem is the carrier and dangerous-goods transport, not the Australian Border Force.
- No 2023–2026 report gave a named last-mile carrier (AusPost, Aramex, CouriersPlease, etc.) for an AliExpress battery parcel.

## Q3. The transport rules behind the logistics (IATA/ICAO, UPU/postal, Australia Post, Australian Border Force)

### Takeaway
LiPos are restricted by **transport (dangerous-goods) rules, not by an Australian import ban**:
- **By air:** stand-alone lithium-ion batteries (UN 3480, PI 965) are **forbidden on passenger aircraft**, **cargo-aircraft-only**, at **≤30% state of charge**, and fully regulated. Since 2022 there has been **no Section II "small battery" exception** for them.
- **By post:** international mail may carry lithium batteries only when **installed in equipment**, at most 4 cells or 2 batteries per package. A loose LiPo cannot legally travel in air mail.
- **Inside Australia:** the Australia Post/StarTrack DG guide (effective 15 July 2026) bans loose UN 3480 batteries from air because of Qantas limitation QF06, and allows them **by road only**, up to 30 kg per package for ≤100 Wh batteries.
- **ABF:** I found no listing of lithium batteries as a prohibited import. A 2025 customs-broker guide and forum comments say no import licence is needed and that the controls are on transport.

### Cited Findings
**IATA / ICAO (air)**
- **[official]** The IATA Battery Guidance Document *"Revised for the 2026 Regulations"* (pages dated 01/01/2026, PDF created 2026-02-05) is based on the 2025–2026 ICAO Technical Instructions and the 67th edition (2026) of the IATA DGR. It says lithium-ion batteries packed by themselves (PI 965) *"a. must be shipped at a state of charge (SoC) not exceeding 30% of their rated capacity… b. cannot be shipped on a passenger aircraft unless approved by the relevant authorities in accordance with Special Provision A201."* — [IATA Battery Guidance Document 2026](https://www.iata.org/contentassets/05e6d8742b0047259bf3a700bc9d42b9/lithium-battery-guidance-document.pdf)
- **[official]** The same document's classification table lists UN 3480 / PI 965 only as **Section IA** (cells >20 Wh or batteries >100 Wh) and **Section IB** (≤20 Wh / ≤100 Wh), each with *"Pax A/C = Forbidden"*, and Section IB at *"CAO = 10 kg"* per package. It has **no Section II for PI 965**. It also says: *"All lithium-ion cells and batteries shipped by themselves (UN 3480) are forbidden for transport as cargo on passenger aircraft. All packages prepared in accordance with Packing Instruction 965, Section IA and IB, must bear a Cargo Aircraft Only label."* — [IATA Battery Guidance Document 2026](https://www.iata.org/contentassets/05e6d8742b0047259bf3a700bc9d42b9/lithium-battery-guidance-document.pdf)
- **[official]** The same document says that from 1 January 2026, lithium-ion batteries **packed with equipment** (PI 966) must also be at ≤30% SoC (Section II: cells or batteries over 2.7 Wh). For batteries **contained in** equipment (PI 967), a reduced SoC is *"strongly recommended"* but not mandatory. — [IATA Battery Guidance Document 2026](https://www.iata.org/contentassets/05e6d8742b0047259bf3a700bc9d42b9/lithium-battery-guidance-document.pdf)
- **[secondary, search summaries]** Section II of PI 965 and PI 968 was removed in the 63rd DGR (effective 1 January 2022), with a transition period to 31 March 2022. Small stand-alone batteries then had to move to Section IA or IB. — [Lion Technology, 2022 DGR lithium updates](https://www.lion.com/lion-news/august-2021/lithium-battery-updates-in-the-2022-iata-dgr); [IHMM, 63rd Edition changes](https://ihmm.org/?p=13946)

**Postal (UPU / Australia Post) — international mail**

### Inferences
- A loose 1S LiPo (about 1.1 Wh) from China or the US to Sydney can legally move only by:
  1. **Cargo-aircraft DG freight** under PI 965 Section IB (≤30% SoC, Class 9 and CAO labels; express couriers usually charge DG fees or refuse), or
  2. **Sea freight** under IMDG, or
  3. A **local AU warehouse** sending it **by road** (AusPost "Road Transport Only" or a road courier).
- **Postal air mail cannot carry it.** This explains BetaFPV's bar on expedited shipping of stand-alone batteries, GetFPV's and RDQ's "ground only" rules, the 2017 Banggood/Gearbest refusals, and the 1–2 month AliExpress sea-freight arrivals in 2025.
- The last mile in Australia must also be by road. Loose UN 3480 batteries cannot enter the AusPost/StarTrack air network (Qantas QF06). Sydney is reachable by road from the east-coast warehouses, so this mostly adds time, not refusals.
- Seizure risk in Australia appears tied to **non-compliant transport**, such as undeclared batteries found by a carrier or airline, rather than to ABF import law. No ABF prohibition on LiPos was found.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the current UPU Convention or Regulations text on lithium batteries. The upu.int page fetched had no lithium content. The 4-cells/2-batteries "installed in equipment" rule is cited via Australia Post's DG Guide and ICAO working papers.
- I found no Australia Post page specific to **inbound** international mail containing loose lithium batteries, such as what happens on detection: return, destruction or road delivery.
- The ABF searchable prohibited-goods list could not be queried, so "not prohibited" rests on the absence of any mention plus secondary sources, not an explicit ABF statement.
- The IATA 2022 Section II removal is cited from secondary sources (Lion Technology, IHMM). The 2026 IATA guidance confirms the current PI 965 structure has no Section II.
