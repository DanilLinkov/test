# Australian (CASA, ACMA) and NSW/Sydney rules for a recreational homebuilt-drone flyer in Sydney — status as at 5 October 2026

_Source-access note for the report writer:_ casa.gov.au, consultation.casa.gov.au, drones.gov.au, acma.gov.au, infrastructure.gov.au, legislation.nsw.gov.au, austlii.edu.au, fire.nsw.gov.au, web.archive.org and archive.ph all failed (503 / empty reply / 403 / connection reset) through both curl-via-proxy and WebFetch on 5 Oct 2026. Therefore: (1) the binding law was read directly from the Federal Register of Legislation (legislation.gov.au), which hosts the CASR, the Part 101 Manual of Standards (MOS), CASA instruments and ACMA class licences — these are the strongest sources below; (2) CASA web-page content is cited only where it came through a search-engine snippet or a verbatim third-party reproduction, and is labelled as such; (3) NSW statute text (Surveillance Devices Act, Crimes Act) could only be cited via NSW government summaries or secondary legal sources. The CASR compilation read is C102 (in force 30 Jun – 28 Sep 2026); the only later amendment (29 Sep 2026) touches large-RPA provisions only (see Q1).

## Q1. CASA recreational rules in force in 2026 (standard rules, ≤250 g allowances, registration/accreditation, Remote ID, FPV, homebuilt)

### Takeaway
A drone flown purely for sport/recreation is legally a "model aircraft", so in October 2026 it needs **no CASA registration and no operator accreditation at any weight** (registration/accreditation apply only to RPA, i.e. non-recreational use); but the Part 101 operating rules apply: ≤400 ft (120 m), within the pilot's own visual line of sight (goggles/screen alone don't count), daytime only, not in restricted areas or controlled-aerodrome no-fly zones, and — above 250 g — 30 m from uninvolved people and not over populous areas. Sub-250 g models get a carve-out near controlled aerodromes. Outdoor FPV-goggle flying is lawful for hobbyists only at MAAA club fields/events under a CASA approval valid to end-August 2027 (with a second person as observer), or indoors. There is no Remote ID requirement; the Government's stated aim is a mandate "by 2030, subject to a regulatory impact analysis".

### Cited Findings

**Which law/version is in force**
- The in-force CASR compilation is F2026C00560 (C102), "30 June 2026 - 28 September 2026"; a further amendment (Civil Aviation Legislation Amendment (Minor Changes) Regulations 2026, F2026L00846) took effect 29 Sep 2026 — [legislation.gov.au CASR versions](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1998B00220/latest/versions).
- That 2026 amendment's Part 101 changes only repeal/substitute reg 101.046 ("Flying without a certificate of airworthiness—RPA") and reg 101.255 ("Conditions on operation of large RPA with certificate of airworthiness") — i.e. large RPA, not recreational flying — [F2026L00846 text](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L00846/asmade/2026-06-29/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html).
- The Part 101 Manual of Standards' latest compilation is F2024C00404 dated 30 April 2024 (no later amendments listed) — [Part 101 MOS versions](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2019L00593/latest/versions).

**Definitions (why a hobby drone is a "model aircraft")**
- CASR 101.023: "A model aircraft is an aircraft (other than a balloon or a kite) that does not carry a person: (a) if the aircraft: (i) is being operated for the purpose of sport or recreation; and (ii) has a gross weight of not more than 150 kg". Note: "A model aircraft is not an RPA" — [CASR Vol 3, C102](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1998B00220/2026-06-30/2026-06-30/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_3/document_3.html).
- CASR 101.022 weight classes for RPA (non-recreational): micro RPA ≤250 g; very small RPA >250 g–2 kg; small RPA >2–25 kg — [CASR Vol 3](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1998B00220/2026-06-30/2026-06-30/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_3/document_3.html).

**Registration and accreditation — not required for recreational (model aircraft)**
- CASR 47.015(1): "an aircraft is required to be registered unless it is one of the following: … (f) a model aircraft" — [CASR Vol 2, C102](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1998B00220/2026-06-30/2026-06-30/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_2/document_2.html).
- CASR 47.096: the drone-registration Division applies to medium, small, very small and **micro** RPA (i.e. non-recreational drones of any weight); registration lasts 12 months (47.099); applicant must be ≥16 (47.097(2)); flying an unregistered RPA that must be registered is a strict-liability offence, 50 penalty units (47.096A) — [CASR Vol 2](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1998B00220/2026-06-30/2026-06-30/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_2/document_2.html).
- CASR 101.374A (accreditation subpart): "This Subpart applies to the operation of excluded RPA and micro RPA. … Note 2: This Subpart does not apply to model aircraft, as they are not RPA"; 101.374B makes flying an excluded/micro RPA without accreditation or a RePL an offence (50 penalty units), with a supervised-under-16 exception — [CASR Vol 3](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1998B00220/2026-06-30/2026-06-30/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_3/document_3.html).
- CONFLICT: a 2026 secondary guide states "Registration with CASA is required for every drone over 250g" and shows "250g – 2kg | Recreational use | Registration required + RPA operator accreditation" — [PickDrones (2026)](https://pickdrones.com/australia-casa-drone-rules/); this is contradicted by the in-force CASR text above (47.015(1)(f); note to 101.374A). Historical context: recreational registration was proposed in 2019 and then deferred ("some years off") — [Inside Imaging (2019, via search)](https://www.insideimaging.com.au/2019/recreational-drone-rules-to-tighten/).

**CASA's published "drone safety rules" for recreational flyers** (casa.gov.au unreachable; wording below is a retailer's verbatim reproduction of CASA's list, original at [CASA drone safety rules (unreachable)](https://www.casa.gov.au/node/566)) — [Sphere Drones reproduction](https://www.spheredrones.com.au/resources/blog/drone-safety-rules):
- "You must not fly your drone higher than 120 metres (400 feet) above ground level."
- "You must keep your drone at least 30 metres away from other people."
- "You must only fly one drone at a time."
- "You must keep your drone within visual line-of-sight. This means always being able to see the drone with your own eyes (rather than through a device, screen or goggles)."
- "You must not fly over or above people or in a populous area. This could include beaches, parks, events, or sport ovals where there is a game in progress."
- "If your drone weighs more than 250 grams, you must fly at least 5.5 kilometres away from a controlled airport, which generally have a control tower at them."
- "If you're near a helicopter landing site or smaller aerodrome without a control tower, you can fly your drone within 5.5 kilometres. If you become aware of manned aircraft nearby, you will have to manoeuvre away and land your drone as quickly and safely as possible."
- "You must only fly during the day and you must not fly through cloud or fog."
- "You must not fly your drone over or near an area affecting public safety or where emergency operations are underway…"
- "Respect personal privacy. Don't record or photograph people without their consent — this may breach other laws."
- "you must not operate your drone in a way that creates a hazard to another aircraft, person or property."

**The underlying CASR rules (all from [CASR Vol 3, C102](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1998B00220/2026-06-30/2026-06-30/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_3/document_3.html))**
- 101.055 (applies to all unmanned aircraft incl. indoors): "A person must not operate an unmanned aircraft in a way that creates a hazard to another aircraft, another person, or property. Penalty: 50 penalty units."
- 101.005(3)(b),(4): Subparts 101.C–101.I "do not apply to the operation of … (b) a model aircraft indoors"; "a flight does not take place indoors if the building in which it takes place has the roof, or 1 or more walls, removed." (Subpart 101.B — the hazard rule — still applies.)
- 101.065: flying in/over a prohibited or restricted area only with the controlling authority's permission; 25 penalty units, strict liability.
- 101.070: above 400 ft AGL in controlled airspace only in an approved area and with an ATC clearance; 50 penalty units.
- 101.073: offence (50 penalty units) if an unmanned aircraft "is not operated within the person's visual line of sight"; VLOS means the person "can continually see, orient and navigate the aircraft … with or without corrective lenses, but without the use of binoculars, a telescope or other similar device." Exception only with a CASA approval under 101.029.
- 101.075: above 400 ft within 3 NM of an aerodrome, or over a movement area/runway or an approach/departure path, only if permitted; must not obstruct aircraft taking off/landing; 25 penalty units.
- 101.085: above 400 ft AGL only in an approved area or as otherwise permitted by Part 101; 50 penalty units.
- 101.095: in cloud, "at night", or other than VMC "only if permitted by another provision of this Part"; 25 penalty units.
- 101.097: the autonomous-aircraft launch prohibition "does not apply to balloons, kites or model aircraft".
- 101.375: Subpart 101.G (model-aircraft rules) "applies to the operation of model aircraft weighing more than 250 g" (and not indoors / control-line).
- 101.385 (>250 g): fly only if visibility lets the operator "see it continuously".
- 101.390 (>250 g): "A person may operate a model aircraft at night only in accordance with the written procedures of an approved aviation administration organisation."
- 101.395 (>250 g): must not fly over a populous area below a height from which it could clear the area if a component fails; a powered model aircraft must stay "at least 30 metres away from anyone not directly associated with the operation of model aircraft" (exceptions: people standing behind at take-off; competition judges); 50 penalty units.
- 101.400 (>250 g): "may operate a model aircraft outside an approved area above 400 feet AGL only if he or she: (a) keeps it in sight; and (b) keeps it clear of populous areas"; 10 penalty units.
- 101.025: a "populous area" is one with "sufficient density of population" that a failure "might … pose an unreasonable risk to the life, safety or property of somebody … not connected with the operation."

**Special allowances for ≤250 g model aircraft (Part 101 MOS, [compilation 30 Apr 2024](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2019L00593/2024-04-30/2024-04-30/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html))**
- MOS 4.02: "no-fly zone of a controlled aerodrome means any areas and airspace that are below 400 ft and: (a) within 3 NM, in any direction, from the measurement point of any runway of a controlled aerodrome; or (b) within the approach and departure paths referred to in section 4.05, whether or not they extend beyond 3 NM".
- MOS 4.03(5): "A person must not fly a model aircraft that has a gross weight of more than 250 g in the no-fly zone of a controlled aerodrome."
- MOS 4.03(6): "A person may fly a model aircraft that has a gross weight of no more than 250 g in the no-fly zone of a controlled aerodrome PROVIDED the aircraft does not enter: (a) an approach and departure path … ; or (b) any area within the aerodrome boundary."
- MOS 4.05(2): the approach and departure path (up to 400 ft) includes the runway/strip from ground up; the black-shaded area from the ground up "to a distance of 7 km from the end of the runway strip"; from 300 ft (90 m) between 7 and 8.5 km; and "(c) anywhere from 150 ft (45 m) above the ground (referenced to the aerodrome elevation) in the area that is shaded grey" — the grey area "extends 3 NM in all directions from the measurement point". The black area is trapezoids from the ends of a nominal 100 m runway strip splaying 15° each side to 8.5 km, plus a rectangle 500 m either side of the runway centreline.
- MOS 9.03(3)(b): near non-controlled aerodromes and helicopter landing sites, ≤250 g model aircraft may keep flying even while a manned aircraft is present, if outside the movement area and approach/departure paths; heavier models must manoeuvre away and land (9.04).
- Consequence of 101.375 (above): the CASR 30 m / populous-area / night-procedure rules in Subpart 101.G legally bind only >250 g models — [CASR Vol 3](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1998B00220/2026-06-30/2026-06-30/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_3/document_3.html) — while CASA's published recreational rules still tell all recreational flyers to keep 30 m from people and not fly over people — [Sphere Drones reproduction of CASA rules](https://www.spheredrones.com.au/resources/blog/drone-safety-rules).

**FPV (goggles) and spotters**
- CASA's FPV page (unreachable; content via search snippet): "You must always keep your drone within your visual line-of-sight… When you fly FPV with goggles or solely by looking at a screen you can't see the drone with your own eyes"; FPV indoors for recreation is possible "provided the drone can't get out of the building, and you do not need CASA approval"; outdoor FPV is via the MAAA, which "can only be conducted at a CASA approved MAAA location" — [CASA "Flying first person view" (via search snippet)](https://www.casa.gov.au/drones/drone-rules/flying-first-person-view).
- Primary instrument: CASA.RPAS.0572 "First Person View Operation (Model Aeronautical Association of Australia) Approval 2022", commences 1 Sep 2022 and "is repealed at the end of August 2027". It approves MAAA affiliate members, education visitors, honorary/prospective members and visitors, "For subregulation 101.073(2) … to operate a model aircraft beyond the person's visual line of sight if the person operates the model aircraft: (a) Using first person view technology; and (b) In accordance with the FPV Policy [MOP066]; and (c) Only at registered MAAA club flying fields or CASA approved MAAA events." — [CASA.RPAS.0572 (hosted by MAAA)](https://www.maaa.asn.au/images/Drones/MAAA_FPV_approval.pdf).
- Earlier FPV exemptions (CASA EX138/16 for MAAA; CASA EX37/17 for the Australian Miniature Aerosports Society) are repealed/expired (effective to 30 Aug 2019 and 28 Feb 2020 respectively) — [EX138/16 details](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2016L01392/latest/details); [EX37/17 details](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2017L00270/latest/details).
- MAAA MOP066 (FPV & Self-Guided Model Aircraft policy, v. 19 Aug 2019), section 8 "For model aircraft under 2kg or operating below 100Ft AGL": "8.1 An FPV equipped Model Aircraft shall be flown by two Affiliate Members"; the observer "shall maintain the model within Visual Control Range and shall be able to immediately assume control of the model in the event of a problem via a handover of the radio" (8.3); "The observer shall be solely responsible for the safety of the flight" (8.5); range check with camera on/off and failsafe enabled (8.7); check the video channel isn't in use before powering a VTX (8.11). Section 7 (>2 kg or >100 ft): buddy-box or functioning Return-to-Home, pilot in command of "Silver Wings standard". MOP066 applies "only … to MAAA Affiliate members flying at MAAA Clubs and Affiliated flying sites" — [MAAA MOP066](https://www.maaa.asn.au/images/MOP066-Policy-FPV-and-SGMA-Policy_190819.pdf).
- MOP066 FPV racing conditions: gates/flags course, "recommended minimum 30m exclusion zone", aircraft "lower than 100m", failsafe must stop all motors — [MAAA MOP066](https://www.maaa.asn.au/images/MOP066-Policy-FPV-and-SGMA-Policy_190819.pdf).
- For RPA (not hobby) EVLOS operations, the MOS says "an FPV system may be used, but not as a substitute for any observer" and "A remote pilot cannot simultaneously use an FPV and be an observer" — [Part 101 MOS](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2019L00593/2024-04-30/2024-04-30/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html).

- The Department of Infrastructure ran a Remote ID discussion paper (submissions closed 28 July 2023) considering Network and Broadcast Remote ID — [Infrastructure Remote ID discussion paper (via search; site unreachable)](https://www.infrastructure.gov.au/department/media/publications/remote-identification-remote-id-discussion-paper-public-consultation).

**Homebuilt / GPS / autonomous**
- No CASR airworthiness or registration requirement attaches to model aircraft (registration exemption 47.015(1)(f)); the RPA modification and identification rules (101.098, 101.099A; MOS Ch. 12 and 14) apply to RPA "registered under Division 47.C.2" — [CASR Vol 3](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1998B00220/2026-06-30/2026-06-30/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_3/document_3.html); [Part 101 MOS](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2019L00593/2024-04-30/2024-04-30/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html).

### Inferences
- **(a) Sub-100 g ESP32 phone-flown micro drones**: indoors (in a fully enclosed building, with the owner's permission) only the general "don't create a hazard" rule applies (101.005(3)(b), 101.055). Outdoors they are ≤250 g model aircraft: ≤400 ft (in practice ≤45 m within 3 NM of a controlled aerodrome), daylight only, eyes-on VLOS (watching the phone's camera feed instead of the drone breaches 101.073), not in restricted areas or approach/departure paths/aerodrome boundaries; CASA's 30 m and no-overflight-of-people rules should be followed even though Subpart 101.G legally binds only >250 g. No registration, no accreditation.
- **(b) Sub-250 g FPV whoops**: legal indoors without CASA approval; outdoors with goggles only as an MAAA affiliate at a registered MAAA club field/approved event, following MOP066 (two affiliate members; observer keeps VLOS and can take over via radio handover). A lone "spotter" in a public park does not make goggle flying lawful, because 101.073 requires the *operator* to keep VLOS and the only recreational BVLOS approval found is CASA.RPAS.0572 (MAAA fields); the alternative is an individual CASA approval under 101.029 (no evidence found that CASA grants these to hobbyists).
- **(c) ~600–700 g 5-inch GPS quad**: a >250 g model aircraft: everything in (a) plus legally binding 30 m separation, no populous areas, no night (unless under MAAA written procedures), and it may not fly anywhere inside a controlled-aerodrome no-fly zone (3 NM ≈ 5.5 km radius plus approach/departure paths out to 8.5 km). FPV only at MAAA fields under MOP066 (under 2 kg → two-person observer model). Still no registration/accreditation while purely recreational.
- If the user ever flies for money (e.g., paid filming), the same airframes become RPA: even a micro RPA (≤250 g) then needs registration (47.096) and accreditation or RePL (101.374B). Whether monetised social-media videos count as "sport or recreation" is not addressed in the text read.
- Night flying for ≤250 g outdoors appears not permitted at all: 101.095 bars night "only if permitted by another provision", and the only night permission (101.390) sits in Subpart 101.G, which applies only to >250 g models.

## Q2. Controlled airspace and no-fly areas around Sydney (5.5 km rule, Sydney/Bankstown/Camden/Richmond/WSI, heliports, apps, Sydney Harbour, national parks)

### Takeaway
"5.5 km" is CASA's plain-English rendering of the MOS 3 NM no-fly zone around controlled aerodromes (plus approach/departure corridors to 8.5 km). Since 9 July 2026 the Sydney Basin has four civil control zones — Western Sydney International (Class C), Sydney (Class C), Bankstown and Camden (Class D) — so a >250 g model may not fly in any of those zones, while a ≤250 g model may, if it stays out of the runway corridors and aerodrome boundary and below 45 m inside 3 NM. Separately, Sydney Harbour is covered by restricted areas R407A/R407B (from 9 July 2026), where hobbyists have no standing permission. Check a CASA-verified app (e.g., OpenSky) before every flight.

### Cited Findings
- No-fly zone geometry and the >250 g ban / ≤250 g conditional allowance: MOS 4.02, 4.03(5)–(6), 4.05 (quoted in Q1) — [Part 101 MOS](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2019L00593/2024-04-30/2024-04-30/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html).
- MOS 4.04 note: controlled aerodromes are designated in the "Determination of airspace and controlled aerodromes etc." (reissued about every 6 months) and also published by Airservices in the Designated Airspace Handbook — [Part 101 MOS](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2019L00593/2024-04-30/2024-04-30/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html).
- Sydney Basin airspace changes effective 9 July 2026: "the Sydney Basin will have four control zones operating side by side: WSI with new Class C airspace, Sydney Kingsford Smith Airport retaining Class C with redefined boundaries, and Bankstown and Camden airports both Class D"; Bankstown's control zone "extends northwest"; "Drone operators should check a CASA-verified drone safety app before flying to confirm where approval is required" — [Australian Flying](https://www.australianflying.com.au/latest/sydney-basin-airspace-overhaul-takes-effect-9-july-what-pilots-need-to-do-now).
- Western Sydney International (Nancy-Bird Walton) Airport: freight operations from Sunday 26 July 2026; first passengers 25 October 2026; 1 November 2026 "intended gazettal date for WSI to be open for night aircraft movements" — [Prime Minister media release](https://www.pm.gov.au/media/its-official-western-sydney-open-passengers-25-october-and-freight-26-july-2026); [Airservices engagement page](https://engage.airservicesaustralia.com/western-sydney-airport/news_feed/wsi-opening-dates-announced).
- Non-controlled aerodromes and helicopter landing sites: "no-fly zone of an HLS means the area and airspace that is a cylinder … radius of 0.75 NM; and … vertical height of 400 ft"; non-controlled aerodrome no-fly zone = within 3 NM or the approach/departure paths; flying is allowed except during a "relevant event" (a manned aircraft in that airspace), when the pilot must manoeuvre away and land (MOS 9.02–9.04) — [Part 101 MOS](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2019L00593/2024-04-30/2024-04-30/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html).
- **Sydney Harbour**: CASA 28/26 (commenced 9 July 2026; repealed end 6 July 2028) concerns restricted areas "YMMM/R407A SYDNEY" and "YMMM/R407B SYDNEY", declared under CASA OAR 088/26, and repealed CASA 67/24 (R405A/R405B). Its permission is only for a "remote pilot" who holds a remote pilot licence and is (or works for) a certified RPA operator, flying "only as an aerial work operation", registered via a CASA-verified app, with a listening watch on 120.8 MHz — [CASA 28/26](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L00946/asmade/2026-07-08/text/original/pdf).
- Explanatory statement: "CASA is mentioned in AIP as being the controlling authority for R407A and R407B"; the total R407A+B area equals the old R405A+B area, and "a greater proportion … has an upper limit of 1 000 ft above mean sea level (AMSL)" (vs 500 ft AMSL before); operating in a restricted area without the controlling authority's permission is a strict-liability offence under 101.065 — [CASA 28/26 explanatory statement](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L00946/asmade/2026-07-08/es/original/pdf).
- Penalty for restricted-area breach: 25 penalty units (CASR 101.065) — [CASR Vol 3](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F1998B00220/2026-06-30/2026-06-30/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_3/document_3.html).
- CASA-verified app definition: an app "connected to the CASA RPAS digital platform, which is listed as a verified app on the CASA website", at casa.gov.au/drones/safety-apps — [CASA 28/26](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L00946/asmade/2026-07-08/text/original/pdf).
- Verified-app list as shown on CASA's page (search snippet; page unreachable): AALTI; Aerologix; AEROphoria (UXMachines); AVCRM's RPA Manager; Curo by Sphere Drones; FlyFreely; Fly.Ylobe (Yarra Drones); ok2fly (Avsoft); OpenSky (Wing) — [CASA verified apps (via search)](https://www.casa.gov.au/droneapp). Earlier CASA additions reported: Aerologix, AirAssess, AVCRM, Flyanra and FlyFreely joined three existing apps; apps "clearly define the location of restricted airspace, major obstacles, airports and danger areas" — [Technology Decisions](https://www.technologydecisions.com.au/content/it-management/news/casa-verifies-five-new-drone-safety-apps-975162425).
- OpenSky is "a free app that supports flight planning for many types of drone users, both recreational and commercial … we'll tailor a flight brief to your operation and drone"; it is a "CASA-verified drone safety app" — [Wing OpenSky](https://wing.com/opensky).
- Automated airspace approvals near controlled aerodromes (the Automated Airspace Authorisation Trial, from 2021) are a pathway for RPA operators, not hobbyists — [CASA 28/26 explanatory statement](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L00946/asmade/2026-07-08/es/original/pdf).
- National parks: "The use of drones is also prohibited in parks that are close to controlled aerodromes, such as Sydney Airport" — [NPWS Drones in parks policy](https://www.environment.nsw.gov.au/topics/parks-reserves-and-protected-areas/park-policies/drones-in-parks).

### Inferences
- With four control zones (Sydney, Bankstown, Camden, WSI) and the harbour restricted areas, large parts of south, south-west, west and inner Sydney are off-limits to the 600–700 g quad; the micro drones and whoops (≤250 g) can legally fly in more places but must avoid runway corridors (out to 8.5 km from runway ends), aerodrome land, and >45 m height within 3 NM.
- Because WSI's Class C control zone took effect on 9 July 2026 (freight from 26 July; passengers from 25 Oct 2026), older maps or app caches from before July 2026 will under-state no-fly areas in western Sydney.
- The 45 m ceiling is measured relative to aerodrome elevation, so on higher ground near an aerodrome the usable height above you is less than 45 m.
- Bankstown and Camden are Class D towers; whether a no-fly zone lapses outside tower hours was not checked — rely on the app on the day.

### Gaps
- RAAF Base Richmond (military, ATC-controlled), Holsworthy military area and Sydney hospital helipads: no primary source located for their exact drone status; check an app.
- The geographic boundaries of R407A/R407B (Schedule 1 of CASA OAR 088/26) were not retrieved.

## Q3. NSW and Sydney land-manager rules, and NSW privacy/surveillance law

### Takeaway
On top of CASA's airspace rules, most big public green spaces in central Sydney ban hobby launches: NPWS parks need written approval (and none near Sydney Airport), all Greater Sydney Parklands sites (Centennial Parklands, Parramatta Park, Western Sydney Parklands, Callan Park, Fernhill) prohibit recreational drones, the Botanic Gardens (Royal Botanic Garden, The Domain, Mount Annan, Mount Tomah) prohibit them, and the City of Sydney does not approve non-commercial drone use on council land. ### Cited Findings

- NSW Surveillance Devices Act 2007: ss 7 and 8 create offences, including "using an optical surveillance to record activities on premises without the consent of the owner or occupier", punishable "by up to five years' imprisonment" — [NSW Department of Communities and Justice paper](https://dcj.nsw.gov.au/content/dam/dcj/dcj-website/documents/legal-and-justice/laws-and-legislation/surveillance-devices-act-2007-and-statutory-powers-to-make-audio-and-video-recordings.pdf). Search snippet of the bill: s 8 applies where use of the device "involves entry onto or into premises or a vehicle without consent" — [NSW Parliament bill (via search)](https://www.parliament.nsw.gov.au/bill/files/1018/LA%206407.pdf).
- Commentary: "While a person can be charged for filming you in your backyard while standing on your property, that law doesn't apply to drones" — [Sydney Criminal Lawyers (older article)](https://www.sydneycriminallawyers.com.au/blog/the-use-of-drones-in-different-situations-should-there-be-greater-legal-clarity/).

- For a beginner in inner Sydney, the realistic outdoor options are: (i) private land with the owner's permission (outside aerodrome no-fly zones and R407A/B), (ii) council parks in LGAs that don't prohibit it (check signage / council each time), or (iii) an MAAA club field. Big "destination" parks (Centennial, Botanic Garden, Domain, Parramatta Park, Western Sydney Parklands) and City of Sydney parks are effectively out.
- A camera micro-drone hovering near neighbours' yards creates civil exposure under the 2025 statutory tort even where NSW's trespass-based surveillance offence might not bite.

## Q4. Radio rules (ACMA): LIPD class licence limits for 2.4 GHz and 5.8 GHz, ExpressLRS regulatory domain, amateur licensing

### Takeaway
Since 1 October 2025 the Radiocommunications (Low Interference Potential Devices) Class Licence **2025** governs licence-free radios. 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi/ESP-NOW/ExpressLRS links are authorised up to 4 W EIRP (frequency hoppers need ≥75 hop channels for 4 W; digital modulation has a 25 mW/3 kHz density cap); a 915–928 MHz link up to 1 W EIRP; but a generic/analog 5.8 GHz video transmitter is limited to **25 mW EIRP within 5725–5875 MHz**. For ExpressLRS in Australia use **ISM_2400** for 2.4 GHz and **AU_915** (not FCC_915) for 900 MHz. Going above LIPD limits needs an amateur qualification (Standard or Advanced for the 2.4/5.8 GHz amateur bands), and amateur use is limited to "amateur purposes".

### Cited Findings
- LIPD Class Licence 2025 "commences at the start of 1 October 2025"; it "authorises a person to operate a radiocommunications transmitter included in a class … subject to the conditions" — [LIPD 2025 (F2025L01047)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2025L01047/asmade/2025-09-05/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html). The 2015 class licence was repealed 1 October 2025 — [LIPD 2015 details](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2015L01438/latest/details).
- Conditions: "must not operate … at a radiated power that exceeds, in any direction, the maximum EIRP for the class" (s 7(2)); must not cause interference (s 8); must meet ARPANSA public exposure limits (s 9); Note 4: using "an external antenna, other than an antenna supplied with the transmitter, may result in a breach"; LIPDs get no protection from interference in the ISM bands incl. 2400–2500 MHz and 5725–5875 MHz — [LIPD 2025](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2025L01047/asmade/2025-09-05/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html).
- Schedule 1, Part 1 (general transmitters, class "Any"): 433.05–434.79 MHz — 25 mW; 915–928 MHz — 3 mW; 2400–2483.5 MHz — 10 mW; **5725–5875 MHz — 25 mW** EIRP — [LIPD 2025](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2025L01047/asmade/2025-09-05/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html).
- Schedule 1, Part 4 Table 4 (telecommand/telemetry): items 8–9 — 2400–2450, 5725–5795 and 5815–5875 MHz at 1 W; 5795–5815 MHz at 2 W — [LIPD 2025](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2025L01047/asmade/2025-09-05/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html).
- Schedule 1, Part 8 Table 8: frequency hopping 915–928 MHz 1 W (cl 39: ≥20 hopping frequencies); FH 2400–2483.5 MHz 500 mW (cl 40: EN 300 328 or ≥15 hops); **FH 2400–2483.5 MHz 4 W and FH 5725–5850 MHz 4 W (cl 41: ≥75 hopping frequencies)**; digital modulation 915–928 MHz 1 W, **2400–2483.5 MHz 4 W**, 5725–5850 MHz 4 W (cl 43: "radiated peak power spectral density" ≤ "25 mW per 3 kHz") — [LIPD 2025](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2025L01047/asmade/2025-09-05/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html).
- The LIPD 2025 text read contains no definition of "digital modulation radiocommunications transmitter" (class labels only) — [LIPD 2025](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2025L01047/asmade/2025-09-05/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html).
- ExpressLRS: regulatory domains offered are AU_915, EU_868, IN_866, FCC_915, ISM_2400, EU_CE_2400 — "enable whatever regulatory domain you are in"; "EU Regulatory domains are now LBT compliant"; site banner "ExpressLRS v4.1.0 is out now" — [ExpressLRS Firmware Options](https://www.expresslrs.org/quick-start/firmware-options/).
- ExpressLRS source: AU915 = 915.5–926.9 MHz with 20 channels; FCC915 = 903.5–926.9 MHz with 40; AU433 = 433.42–434.42 MHz with 3; ISM2G4 (and CE_LBT) = 2400.4–2479.4 MHz with 80 channels — [ExpressLRS FHSS.cpp](https://github.com/ExpressLRS/ExpressLRS/blob/master/src/lib/FHSS/FHSS.cpp).
- Older guidance claiming high-power 5.8 GHz FPV is fine in Australia relied on the 2000 class licence; a commenter notes "the new law is 25mw for 5.8ghz" and argues FPV could be treated as "telemetry" — [FliteTest (old article and comments)](https://www.flitetest.com/articles/Legal_FPV_frequencies_in_Australia).
- MAAA MOP066 requires FPV flyers to comply with "the ACMA Class Licence for the frequency band being used for the video down link" — [MAAA MOP066](https://www.maaa.asn.au/images/MOP066-Policy-FPV-and-SGMA-Policy_190819.pdf).
- Amateur Stations Class Licence 2023 "commences at the start of 19 February 2024"; a person "must not operate an amateur station unless the person is: (a) a qualified person; or (b) supervised by a qualified person"; qualified = recognised qualification or ACMA recognition certificate (Foundation/Standard/Advanced) — [Amateur Stations Class Licence 2023 (F2023L01648)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2023L01648/asmade/2023-12-12/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html).
- Bands (Schedule 2): Foundation — HF segments, 144–148 MHz and 430–450 MHz at 10 W pX (no 2.4 or 5.8 GHz); Standard — adds 1.24–1.3 GHz, **2.400–2.450 GHz and 5.650–5.850 GHz** at 100 W pX (J3E/R3E) or 30 W pY otherwise; Advanced — 2.4–2.45 GHz and 5.65–5.85 GHz among others at 400 W pX / 120 W pY — [Amateur Class Licence 2023](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2023L01648/asmade/2023-12-12/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html).
- Amateur conditions: "must not operate an amateur station otherwise than for an amateur purpose", defined as "(a) self-training in radiocommunications; (b) intercommunication; (c) technical investigations of radiocommunications; (d) transmitting news and information related to the operation of amateur stations"; no financial gain; no "signal that is encoded, for the purpose of obscuring the meaning" (exceptions incl. controlling an unattended amateur station); call sign must be transmitted at the beginning and end of each transmission and "at least once every 10 minutes" — [Amateur Class Licence 2023](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2023L01648/asmade/2023-12-12/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_1/document_1.html).
- How to qualify: clubs with "Amateur Radio trainers, and assessors who are accredited by ACMA to conduct examinations"; passing lets you "apply to ACMA" for a recognition certificate; Foundation exam = 25 multiple-choice questions, 70% pass; "There is no yearly fee for the Amateur Radio operators class licence"; one-time costs about $48 (ACMA recognition certificate) and ~$32 (standard callsign) — [AHARS (SA club; secondary)](https://ahars.au/get-licensed/).

### Inferences
- **ESP32 Wi-Fi / ESP-NOW phone control** uses Wi-Fi digital modulation in 2.4 GHz → fits Table 8 item 7 (4 W EIRP); a stock ESP32 module is far below that.
- **ExpressLRS 2.4 GHz**: ISM_2400 (80 hop channels) fits the FH 4 W class (≥75 hops). EIRP includes antenna gain: e.g., 1 W (30 dBm) into an 8 dBi patch ≈ 38 dBm ≈ 6.3 W EIRP, over the 4 W (36 dBm) cap. EU_CE_2400 (LBT) is not needed in Australia.
- **ExpressLRS 900 MHz**: AU_915 (915.5–926.9 MHz, 20 hops) fits the 915–928 MHz FH 1 W class; **FCC_915 transmits down to 903.5 MHz, outside the LIPD band, so it is not authorised** in Australia. AU433 is limited to 25 mW EIRP licence-free (433 MHz higher power would need an amateur qualification and must fit "amateur purpose").
- **Analog 5.8 GHz VTX**: under the general "Any" class, 25 mW EIRP max and the whole emission must sit inside 5725–5875 MHz — so commonly sold channels like Raceband R1 (5658), R2 (5695), R7 (5880), R8 (5917) and the low/E bands fall outside regardless of power; "PIT/25 mW" mode on in-band channels (e.g., R3–R6) is the licence-free setting. The "it's telemetry so 1–2 W" argument is not supported by the instrument text (video is not obviously telecommand/telemetry).
- **Digital FPV systems** (DJI O3/O4, Walksnail, HDZero): whether they qualify as "digital modulation" (4 W, 5725–5850 MHz, PSD ≤25 mW/3 kHz) or fall back to the 25 mW general class is unclear without ACMA guidance or the manufacturer's Australian compliance data.
- An amateur qualification (Standard or Advanced) unlocks higher-power 5.65–5.85 GHz video, but legality for FPV hinges on the "amateur purpose" wording and call-sign identification every 10 minutes (e.g., callsign in the OSD) — treat as uncertain and confirm with ACMA.

### Gaps
- ACMA's website (drone/FPV guidance, amateur assessment pages, recognised-qualification list) was unreachable; no ACMA statement on FPV video or on DJI/Walksnail/HDZero classification was found.
- The supply/labelling (RCM) obligations for personally imported transmitters were not researched (MAAA FAQ notes ACMA accepts certain FCC/ETSI standards listed in MAAA MOP058 — [MAAA FAQ](https://maaa.asn.au/membership/faqs)).
- Sydney-based ACMA-accredited amateur assessors were not identified.

## Q6. Safety and lithium-battery guidance from NSW authorities

### Takeaway
NSW Government and Fire and Rescue NSW treat lithium-ion batteries as a fast-rising fire risk: charge attended, on hard non-flammable surfaces, away from exits, with a working smoke alarm and matched chargers; stop using swollen/damaged packs; never put batteries in household or kerbside bins — use B-cycle drop-offs, Community Recycling Centres or Household Chemical CleanOut events, taping terminals.

### Cited Findings
- FRNSW (via NSW EPA media release, 10 Feb 2025): "at least 318 lithium-ion battery fires in NSW last year, that's well up on the 272 of 2023 and 165 of 2022"; leading causes include incorrect chargers and modified devices; advice: don't overcharge, don't charge while asleep, don't "mix and match power cords", "Don't charge lithium-ion-powered devices near exit points"; campaign "Shop, Charge and Recycle Safely" — [NSW EPA media release](https://www.epa.nsw.gov.au/news/epamedia/250210-spate-of-lithium-ion-fires-and-nsw-government-survey-sparks-community-wakeup-call-on-battery-risks).
- NSW Government guidance: "Always use a charger and battery that are compatible"; "Charge batteries on hard, non-flammable surfaces like concrete or tiles, away from doorways and exits"; "Charge batteries in a room with a working smoke alarm"; "Never leave batteries to charge unattended"; "Allow time for batteries to cool down after use before you charge or store them"; "Monitor charging times and disconnect once the battery is full"; stop using batteries showing "swelling or bulging, leaking, cracks, dents, punctures, or crushing" — [NSW Government lithium-ion battery safety](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/safety-home/electrical-safety/lithium-ion-batteries-and-e-micromobility-devices).
- Damaged batteries: "Never throw a damaged battery in your regular home waste bin, recycling bin or kerbside bin"; keep it "at least 3 metres away from any structures or combustible materials" until disposal; contact "a B-cycle accredited battery collector" or the NSW EPA Environment Line — [NSW Government](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/safety-home/electrical-safety/lithium-ion-batteries-and-e-micromobility-devices).
- Disposal: "Do not dispose of lithium-ion batteries in any household or kerbside bin"; removable batteries can go to "B-cycle battery recycling drop off points", "Community Recycling Centres", "Household Chemical CleanOut events"; for small batteries "Cover the terminals with clear, adhesive tape to prevent sparking" — [NSW Government](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/safety-home/electrical-safety/lithium-ion-batteries-and-e-micromobility-devices).
- NSW Fair Trading mandatory safety standards for e-bikes/e-scooters/e-skateboards/hoverboards started 1 February 2025 (not drones) — [NSW EPA media release](https://www.epa.nsw.gov.au/news/epamedia/250210-spate-of-lithium-ion-fires-and-nsw-government-survey-sparks-community-wakeup-call-on-battery-risks).

### Inferences
- For LiPo packs used in whoops and the 5-inch: apply the same NSW advice — balance-charge on a non-flammable surface (fireproof bag/ammo box), never overnight or unattended, let packs cool after flights, retire puffed packs, and tape terminals before taking them to a B-cycle point. NSW e-micromobility standards don't cover hobby LiPos, so charger quality is the user's responsibility.

### Gaps
- Fire and Rescue NSW's own website (incl. its "chargesafe" PDF) returned 403, so FRNSW advice is cited via the EPA release and the NSW Government page; no NSW guidance specific to hobby LiPo/drone batteries was found.
