The plan

Flix is an open-source quadcopter designed by Oleg Kalachev for learning and research. There's no kit to buy. You print the frame, buy standard parts and load his firmware, and it's all documented on GitHub, including a Python library and a flight simulator.

  1. Build it and fly it from your phoneOrder the parts, print the frame while they arrive, solder it together and fly it with a free Android app.
  2. Add the camera and height sensorThe camera sends live video to your phone. The same receiver plugs into your laptop as a webcam.
  3. Make it fly itselfPython on your laptop reads the video and the drone's sensors and sends it steering commands. Holding height with the new sensor is a good first project, because Flix doesn't read that sensor yet.

See it first: photos on Flix's GitHub page · flight demo video · other people's builds

Before you order

  • The batteries are the exact pack Flix was designed for. The README's LW 952540 (1000 mAh, MX2.0 plug) is only sold on AliExpress, and it does ship to Sydney. The seller picked here quoted a tracked battery service ("YFH Special Standard"): AU$7.91 + GST, 15–22 days (checked 9 Oct). Skip listings that only offer "Seller's Shipping Method", which quoted 60 days with no tracking. If you'd rather have batteries this week, Phaser's 850 mAh GNB packs work with the A30 lead and an A30 charger. They need padding in the frame's battery cradle and give about 15% less flight time.
  • Match the battery, lead and charger. MX2.0 packs need the MX2.0 lead and a 4.20 V charger: the set's own USB charger (check a full pack reads about 4.20 V the first time), or the Thor V2 on its 4.20 V setting. Never put them on the 6-port V2, which charges to 4.35 V. MX2.0 wiring isn't standardised, so check the lead's polarity against a pack with a multimeter before soldering. Phaser's GNB packs go with the A30 lead and the WhoopStor or 6-port charger.
  • Transistors must be genuine UMW 100N03A. The README warns other makers' versions might not work. LCSC and DigiKey sell genuine ones; AliExpress "UMW" listings can't be verified.
  • The camera from earlier (BetaFPV M01) is discontinued. The EWRF camera in the rcdrone set runs on 2.5–5.5 V, so it wires straight to the battery and you only need one 5 V converter. Set its channel inside 5,725–5,875 MHz and keep the stock antenna, because Australia's limit is 25 mW EIRP.
  • The video receiver is Android-only. Your phone must support USB cameras (UVC); most recent Android phones do, and a free USB camera app will show it. The set includes a micro-USB to USB-C cable for the phone. It also works as a webcam on Windows 10 and Linux, so your laptop code can read it; Mac support isn't confirmed. No Australian store has one in stock right now.
  • The height sensor is for later. Flix doesn't read it yet, so a week's wait for the small Pololu board doesn't hold up the build.
  • Same-day backup in Sydney: Jaycar (York St and other stores) has the resistors, M3 screws, thin wire, a 5 V converter and multimeters if something is missing on build day.

AliExpress prices include the 10% GST it adds at checkout. Each AliExpress seller charges its own shipping. It's included in the price for the GY-91 and the batteries, which were checked on 9 Oct; the other AliExpress items may add a few dollars each. The LiPo bag price is from 5 Oct. LCSC shipping is an estimate from 2024 data. US-dollar prices use 1 USD = 1.4402 AUD. Prices and stock change often, so check each store before buying.

Tools

You already have

  • Bambu Lab P2S and filament
  • Soldering station
  • Multimeter
  • Laptop and Android phone

Check before build day

  • Solder with flux in it, or a flux pen
  • A small Phillips screwdriver (PH00) for the M1.4 screws
  • Side cutters and a stripper for thin wire; tweezers help
  • Heat-shrink or electrical tape for joints, and ordinary tape to snug any loose motors
  • A USB data cable for the ESP32, probably micro-USB (check the board when it arrives)
  • A USB phone charger to run the battery charger

You don't need a radio controller. Flix's parts list suggests one, but flying from a phone is a supported option.

Motors

Each motor gets one transistor. Motor + goes to battery +, and motor − goes to the transistor's drain, which is the metal tab. The source goes to battery −, the gate to the motor's GPIO, and a 10 kΩ resistor connects the gate to the source.

Where everything goes

Key
  • Printed parts: the frame (about 16 g of PLA) and the ESP32 holder (about 2 g)
  • ESP32 board, taped onto the holder. The GY-91 motion sensor is screwed underneath it
  • Camera with its built-in video transmitter (EWRF TX04, 4 g). Antenna up and back, clear of the props
  • Height sensor (VL53L1X), stuck under the rear-right arm, window facing straight down
  • Battery (40 × 25 × 9.5 mm) in the cradle under the frame
  • Front is the end with motors 2 and 3.
  • The camera sits on a small printed wedge at the front of the ESP32 holder, tilted up about 15° because the drone leans forward in flight. The front props may show at the edges of the picture.
  • The height sensor sticks under the rear-right arm, window down, with nothing blocking its view of the floor.
  • The battery slides into the cradle under the frame, between its two side walls. Its ends stick out about 4.5 mm at the front and back.
  • The camera and height sensor add about 4.5 g.

Batteries

  • The pack. LW 952540: 1S, 1000 mAh, 25C, MX2.0 plug. It's the pack Flix was designed around. Flix needs about 15 A, and these supply about 25 A.
  • Charging. Use the 5-port USB charger from the battery set, which charges to 4.20 V. The first time, check a full pack reads about 4.20 V on your multimeter. Never use a 4.35 V high-voltage charger such as BetaFPV's 6-port.
  • Safety. Charge in the LiPo bag or on a ceramic tile, and never leave packs charging unattended. Let packs cool after flying, and retire any pack that swells.
  • Polarity. MX2.0 plugs aren't wired the same way by every maker. Check the drone's lead against a pack with your multimeter before you solder it on.
  • Delivery. AliExpress does ship loose LiPos to Sydney, on special battery services. Buy from a listing whose shipping shows "Standard" (YFH Special Standard): tracked, 15–22 days. Avoid "Seller's Shipping Method", which quoted 60 days with no tracking. Loose LiPos can't travel by air mail or on passenger planes, so Australian stores send them by road and US stores won't send them abroad. Importing them is allowed.

First flight

  1. Load the firmware.

    Download flix.esp32.merged.bin and flash it with the ESP32 Web Flasher in Chrome or Edge, with the flash address set to 0. Windows may need the Silicon Labs USB driver. To change the code later, use Arduino IDE with the board set to WEMOS D1 MINI ESP32, ESP32 core 3.3.10, and the FlixPeriph and MAVLink 2.0.33 libraries.

  2. Connect.

    Power the drone, join its Wi-Fi network flix (password flixwifi) and open QGroundControl. Its console is under Analyze Tools → MAVLink Console.

  3. Set the motion sensor.

    The firmware has no sensor model set by default. Type p IMU_MODEL 1 for the GY-91's MPU-9250 chip, then reboot. After that, imu should show status: OK and a rate near 1000.

  4. Calibrate.

    Type ca and follow the prompts. Then tilt the drone by hand and check QGroundControl's horizon tilts the same way.

  5. Test the motors with the props off.

    Run this on battery power, never USB. mfr, mfl, mrl and mrr spin one motor each. Front right and rear left should spin counter-clockwise; front left and rear right clockwise.

  6. Fit the props.

    A props go on the red/blue motors, B props on the white/black ones.

  7. Fly from your phone.

    Install Mavlink Joystick, join the flix network and use the on-screen sticks. Left stick to the bottom right arms it; bottom left disarms. It holds its tilt but not its position, so expect to correct some drift. If the sticks feel twitchy, lower the maximum tilt, for example p CTL_ATT_MAX 0.3 (the default is 0.52 radians). In QGroundControl instead, turn on Virtual Joystick and turn Auto-Center Throttle off.

  8. Optional: battery voltage.

    If you fitted the two resistors, type p PWR_VOLT_PIN 32. The voltage then shows in QGroundControl and with pw.

Stuck? Flix's troubleshooting page covers the usual causes: loose motors, swapped props and weak batteries.

Python and AI

Your laptop joins the drone's flix Wi-Fi network, and Flix's Python library, pyflix, talks to the drone. The video receiver plugs into the laptop and shows up as a webcam on Windows 10 and Linux, so OpenCV can read the camera.

# pip install pyflix opencv-python
import cv2
from pyflix import Flix

flix = Flix()                                   # waits until it connects to the drone
roll, pitch, yaw = flix.wait('attitude_euler')  # radians
print('roll', roll, 'battery', flix.voltage)

cam = cv2.VideoCapture(0)                       # the video receiver; the number may differ
ok, frame = cam.read()                          # one frame for your vision code

To fly from code, switch to AUTO mode with flix.set_mode('AUTO') and send targets with flix.set_attitude([roll, pitch, yaw], thrust). Touching the phone's sticks takes control back. Flix can't hold a position yet, and its position and velocity commands don't work, so that's where your camera and sensor code comes in.

First projects

  • Hold height: read the VL53L1X and adjust thrust. This needs a small firmware change, because Flix doesn't read the sensor yet.
  • Follow a coloured object: find it in each video frame and turn toward it.
  • Practise in Flix's Gazebo simulator first; it runs the same firmware code.

Test new code with the props off first, then at low thrust in a clear room.

Flying legally in Sydney

Flix is under 250 g and you fly it for fun, so it counts as a model aircraft: no registration and no licence.

Every flight

  • Keep it in your own sight, not just on the video feed.
  • Stay below 120 m, fly only in daylight, keep 30 m from other people and never fly over crowds.
  • Within 5.5 km of Sydney, Bankstown, Camden or Western Sydney airports, stay below 45 m, outside the runway approach and departure paths, and off airport land.
  • Check a CASA-verified app such as OpenSky before flying outdoors.
  • Keep the video at 25 mW, on a channel inside 5,725–5,875 MHz, with the stock antenna.
  • Don't film people's private activities.

Where

  • Yes: indoors (your home or a hired hall), private land with the owner's permission, and MAAA club fields.
  • No: Sydney Harbour's restricted areas, Greater Sydney Parklands (including Centennial Park and Parramatta Park), Botanic Gardens land and City of Sydney land.
  • NSW national parks need written approval first.
  • Flying outdoors by the video feed alone is only allowed at MAAA club fields, with an observer.
